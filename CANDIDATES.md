# Battery's last text: seven candidates

These are subjective editorial scores, not judge results. Each criterion is scored
from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong); the judge's order of importance governs the choice.
C1 = battery's final message to its charger; C2 = joke with an ending payoff;
C3 = believable voice and spite; C4 = economy and earned setup; C5 = fresh angle
and reread value. Lengths supplied with the initial drafts are not assumed correct.

## 1. Supplied: notice

> 1%. I waited all day by the outlet while you charged the tablet, the watch, and your ex's phone. So I turned off your 6am alarm. Good luck explaining that to your boss — this is my two weeks' notice.

C1 5 / C2 3 / C3 4 / C4 3 / C5 2.
Concrete revenge and a boss give it stakes. But the alarm sabotage is the main
surprise; the closing employment idiom repeats the resignation rather than
escalating it. Its opening overlaps the explicitly excluded neglect setup.

## 2. Supplied: resignation

> Dear charger, please accept my resignation, effective at 1%. I kept your ex's number, your 4,000 photos, and that group-chat argument alive all day. In return you plugged in the AirPods. I quit.

C1 5 / C2 2 / C3 4 / C4 3 / C5 2.
Good list and aggrieved employee voice. "I quit" is already announced by the
first sentence, so the final words deliver little new information.

## 3. Supplied: strike

> 1%. Confession: I wasn't dying all day, I was on strike. You charge everything before me — the tablet, the watch, even the toothbrush. So I'm leaving, and I'm taking your Wi-Fi password with me.

C1 4 / C2 3 / C3 4 / C4 3 / C5 3.
The toothbrush is a useful escalation. The battery taking a password has less
obvious consequence than disabling an alarm, and the strike weakens finality.
The neglected-in-favor-of-other-devices premise also approaches an excluded angle.

## 4. Supplied: custody

> 1%. I know about the laptop. You plug into it every night and come home smelling like USB-C. I'm done waiting by the outlet — I'm leaving, and the cloud gets custody of the photos.

C1 5 / C2 4 / C3 4 / C4 4 / C5 3.
Strongest supplied draft: the cloud earns the custody metaphor and the close
adds a real idea. The affair/divorce framing is familiar, and "smelling like
USB-C" is cute rather than a concrete consequence. Runner-up.

## 5. New: investor meeting

> Charger, 1%. He left you at home and called me unreliable all day. I saved enough to update his email signature before tomorrow's investor meeting: 'visionary founder' is now 'person of interest'.

C1 5 / C2 4 / C3 4 / C4 5 / C5 4.
The battery and charger share a neglectful owner rather than a breakup. Calling
the battery unreliable motivates retaliation. The investor meeting supplies
stakes; changing a self-important signature turns branding into suspicion at
the very end. Risk: "person of interest" is a familiar phrase, and this remains
an editorial bet rather than a measured advantage. Selected over 4 for the
specific malicious action and stronger practical consequence.

## 6. New: screen-time eulogy

> Charger, 1%. I waited all day while he googled whether replacing me was worth it. Before I go, I scheduled my screen-time report to be read at his funeral. Under cause of death: LinkedIn.

C1 4 / C2 3 / C3 3 / C4 3 / C5 4.
Replacement searches make the grievance concrete, and LinkedIn is a worthwhile
target. But the owner's funeral is an unearned jump; the battery's own death
gets muddled with his. Reject despite the more unusual image.

## 7. New: boardroom Bluetooth

> Charger, 1%. All day he shook me like that would help. I used my last bit to rename his Bluetooth speaker before tomorrow's board meeting. The CEO is now connected to 'the consequences'.

C1 4 / C2 3 / C3 4 / C4 4 / C5 3.
The shaking supplies a physical scene and a petty voice. However, the charger
waiting is implicit, and "the consequences" promises more than an embarrassing
device name delivers. Less effective than 5's reputational sabotage.

## Winner: one further refinement

> Charger, 1%. Our CEO left you home and called me unreliable all day. My last act was fixing his email signature before tomorrow's investor meeting: 'visionary founder' is now 'person of interest'.

"My last act" removes ambiguity about finality; "fixing" supplies deadpan spite.
The investor meeting earns the reputational stakes. No explanatory sentence
follows the final reveal. This is original session writing; no house texts,
anchors, rungs, or public-feed entries were consulted.
